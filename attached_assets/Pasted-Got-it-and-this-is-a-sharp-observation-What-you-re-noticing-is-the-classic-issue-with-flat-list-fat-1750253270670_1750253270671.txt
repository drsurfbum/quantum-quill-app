What you’re noticing is the classic issue with flat list fatigue: once your world grows past a dozen entries, even well-tagged elements become cognitively heavy to browse or organize. Switching from a list view to a bento-style layout would solve that by transforming your Story Bible into a more visual, modular, and navigable experience.

Here’s a structured breakdown of what a Bento-Style Story Bible Layout could look like—and how it improves both organization and usability:

⸻

📦 Quantum Quill – Bento Layout for Story Bible

⸻

🧱 1. Overview Philosophy

Think of each major element category (Characters, Locations, Events, World Elements, etc.) as its own tile or card in a grid layout. These tiles act like visual entry points into that section’s sub-content.

Rather than a single vertical sidebar list, you see a full-page canvas divided into modular blocks—each of which can expand, scroll, or show preview data.

⸻

🧭 2. Top-Level Layout Structure

A clean, 3–4 column grid. Each tile represents one major category. Each tile contains:
	•	Category Title
(e.g. “Characters,” “World Elements,” “Events”)
	•	Icon or Visual Marker
Stylized symbols or emojis (e.g. 🧍‍♂️, 🌍, 📜)
	•	Quick Summary Stats
“22 characters” / “4 religions” / “12 tech items”
	•	Smart Preview Row
	•	Most recently edited
	•	Most tagged or most linked
	•	Manual pinning for “featured” items
	•	[ + Add New ] button inside the tile, not at top level

⸻

📚 3. Sub-Tile Structure for World Elements

Within the World Elements tile, show a mini bento grid:
	•	Each category (Flora, Fauna, Tech, etc.) gets its own sub-card or expandable module.
	•	Cards can be:
	•	Collapsible by default (accordion)
	•	Color-coded like in your Notion setup
	•	Show counts (“Fauna: 6 entries”)
	•	Include pinned or “starred” entries

Optional Smart Filter Toggles:
Add dropdowns or toggles at the top of the Story Bible to dynamically re-sort all tiles by:
	•	Recent Edits
	•	Category
	•	Character associations
	•	Timeline relevance

⸻

⸻

✨ 5. Features This Enables
	•	Faster scanning across major systems (especially helpful during writing or GMing)
	•	Better grouping of elements that otherwise live flatly side-by-side
	•	Flexible mental modeling—users will see their world’s systems evolve
	•	Fewer clicks: contextual access to recent or important entries from the homepage

⸻

🧰 Bonus Ideas
	•	📍 Pin to Board: Let users pin key elements to a “Now in Use” section or dashboard
	•	🔗 Link Preview Hover: When hovering a name in another entry, show a quick card preview
	•	🌐 Map Mode: Long-term—allow Locations and World Elements to be layered onto a literal or conceptual map
