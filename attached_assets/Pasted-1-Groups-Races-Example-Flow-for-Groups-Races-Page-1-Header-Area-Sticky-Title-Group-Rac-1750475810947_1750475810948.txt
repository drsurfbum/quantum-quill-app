1. Groups & Races

Example Flow for Groups & Races Page
	1.	Header Area (Sticky)
	•	Title: Group/Race Name (e.g., “Solar Peace Alliance”)
	•	Badge(s): Category (e.g., “Institution” or “Species”), Status (Active/Defunct)
	•	Save/Delete icons on the right.
	2.	Main Column
	•	Basic Info (expanded initially):
	•	Name
	•	Category (single-select: Species, Faction, Clan, Government, Guild)
	•	Short Notes / Tagline
	•	Hierarchy & Structure (collapsible):
	•	Parent Group (link selector)
	•	Sub-groups or child races (list with “+ Add”)
	•	Founding Date / Era Established
	•	Dissolution Date if defunct
	•	Leadership & Membership (collapsible):
	•	Notable Members (link to Characters; role tags like “Leader,” “Member”)
	•	Leadership Structure (text or structured list)
	•	Attributes & Traits (collapsible):
	•	For Species: biology notes (homeworld, physiology)
	•	For Organization: purpose, ideology, resources
	•	For all: key characteristics or cultural notes
	•	History & Events (collapsible):
	•	Freeform description of origin, major milestones
	•	Link to Event entries (e.g., “Founded in Treaty of X,” “Alliance Wars”)
	•	Relationships (collapsible):
	•	Alliances, rivalries, dependencies (link other Groups/Races)
	•	Notes / Internal Comments (collapsed by default):
	•	Writer’s ideas, future plot hooks, open questions.
	3.	Sidebar
	•	Classification:
	•	Category (Species/Organization/etc.), Status
	•	Affiliations / Links:
	•	Home Location(s) (link to Locations)
	•	Related World Elements (e.g., technologies they use, religious rites)
	•	Tags (chips): e.g., “Mercenary,” “Rebel,” “Ancient”
	•	Timestamps & Actions:
	•	Created/Updated dates (small, de-emphasized)
	•	Archive/Deactivate toggle
	4.	Post-Save Suggestions
	•	Prompt: “View related Characters or Events?”
	•	Offer: “Create a stub Character if you referenced someone not yet defined.”

Prioritization (≤150 words)

Groups & Races form the backbone of relational context: they anchor Characters, shape Events, and influence Locations and World Elements. Initially focus on: name, category (species vs. faction), short summary, and parent/child hierarchy. Ensure easy linking to Characters (members, leaders) and key Events (founding, conflicts). Capture status (active/defunct) early so dashboards can filter. Defer deep cultural or biology fields until you have enough entries to see patterns; keep history and attributes as collapsible for later expansion. Strong relational linking here pays dividends: e.g., viewing all Characters in a faction or all Events involving a race. Establishing a clear taxonomy and linking approach at the outset prevents fragmentation as your world grows.
