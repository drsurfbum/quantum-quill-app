1. Objectives & Principles
	1.	Clarity & Scanability
	•	Ensure that as your world grows, entries remain easy to find and understand at a glance.
	•	Avoid a single, interminable flat list; instead, group and nest entries logically.
	2.	Consistency & Controlled Vocabulary
	•	Use predefined category lists and subcategory options wherever possible.
	•	When users introduce a new term, prompt to confirm and add to the master taxonomy.
	3.	Modularity & Extensibility
	•	Design categories so new subcategories or types can be slotted in without reworking the entire structure.
	•	Permit “Other / Custom” but encourage users to align with existing terms.
	4.	Relational Linking
	•	Every element type (Character, Location, Event, World Element, etc.) should support links to other types.
	•	Categories help filter and make these links meaningful (e.g., linking only a “Technology” world element to relevant Events or Locations).
	5.	User-Centric Entry Flow
	•	When creating or editing an entry, guide the user through selecting a category/subcategory early, so subsequent fields adapt to that choice.
	•	Provide short descriptions or tooltips for each category to reduce confusion.
	6.	Dashboard Organization
	•	Surface high-level overviews (“bento” tiles or cards) so users can quickly jump into a subset of elements rather than scrolling a monolithic list.
